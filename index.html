Ingredients:

  • 500g boneless chicken
  • 2 medium onions, finely chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, pureed
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • Spices (turmeric, cumin, garam masala, salt)

Recipe Steps:

Follow the steps below and you might be able to mimic my recipe as it is:

  • Heat oil in a heavy-bottomed pan and sauté thinly sliced onions until they turn a rich golden brown.
  • Stir in fresh ginger-garlic paste and whole spices until fragrant, then add the chicken pieces and sear them on high heat until they change color.
  • Mix in chopped tomatoes, yogurt, and ground spices—such as cumin, coriander, red chili, and turmeric—before covering the pan to let the chicken simmer in its own juices until tender.
  • Finally, uncover the pot and bhunao (roast) the masala over medium-high heat, stirring continuously until the moisture evaporates, the gravy turns thick and glossy, and the oil separates at the edges, finishing with fresh coriander, green chilies, and a sprinkle of garam masala.

FAQs

Q1) What does "bhuna" mean?
"Bhuna" comes from the cooking technique of roasting or frying spices, aromatics, and meat over controlled heat while evaporating excess moisture. Constant stirring caramelizes the masala and concentrates flavor without drowning the dish in broth.

Q2) How much water should I add?
Use very little water—or none at all if tomatoes and yogurt provide enough moisture. If adding a splash or up to a cup of water to help tenderize the chicken, cook uncovered or partially covered at the end until the excess liquid evaporates and oil rises to the top.

Q3) What pairs best with bhuna chicken?

Serve it hot with naan, roti, or paratha to scoop up the thick masala, along with sliced onions, lemon wedges, or fresh cilantro.
